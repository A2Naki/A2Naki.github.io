Frequently asked

Short answers

The honest version, including the parts we can’t promise.

Is it safe? What about the terms of service?

The app is passive and read-only. It only listens to data the game server already sends to your PC. It never sends anything to the game, never changes game files, never reads game memory and never injects anything.

The risk is real, though: NCSOFT may still consider reading game traffic a breach of the AION 2 terms of service, and that could put your game account at risk — up to a ban. Tools like this are widely used, but there is no guarantee. You decide whether to use it.

Why does it need Npcap?

Npcap is a free network driver (used by tools like Wireshark) that lets a program receive a copy of the network traffic on your PC. A2Naki Companion uses it receive-only. You install it once, from npcap.com; it needs administrator rights because it is a driver.

Keep the default options and leave “Restrict Npcap driver’s access to Administrators only” unticked, otherwise the app can’t use it.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”

That’s Windows SmartScreen. The setup is not code-signed yet (a signing certificate costs money a free fan project doesn’t have), so Windows warns about it. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Only do this for a copy you got from the official release page, and check it against the published SHA256SUMS.txt.

Do I need a Xeneon Edge?

No. The Xeneon Edge version is three page widgets in iCUE — Group, Progress / World and Performance — each filling one page of the 2560 × 720 touchscreen.

The browser page shows pages 1 and 2 together in one Chrome or Edge window, with the same live data and the same look. It fits the window: three columns on a wide screen, two on a tablet held upright, one on a narrow window. Put it on a second monitor, a laptop, or a tablet next to your keyboard. CPU, GPU and FPS need iCUE, so they only show on the Edge.

How does the meter overlay work?

Right-click the A2Naki icon in the tray and choose Meter overlay → Show overlay, or press Ctrl+Shift+M in the game. A small window with the damage meter appears on top of the game. While it is locked, every click goes through it to the game; Ctrl+Shift+1–8 switch between Damage, rDPS, Healing, Taken, Skills, Utility, History and Summary.

To move or resize it, tap the padlock (or choose Unlock overlay in the tray menu), drag the top bar or the edges, then lock it again. Opacity is set from the same menu.

The game has to run in Borderless or Windowed mode — nothing can be drawn over exclusive full screen. The overlay uses Microsoft’s WebView2, which comes with Windows 11 and Microsoft Edge.

How do I practise my rotation on the training dummy?

Hit a Punching Bag or a Training Scarecrow (the melee one too). The target card in the Encounter turns into a training card: pick a length from 30 seconds to 5 minutes before you start — the clock starts on your first hit — and watch your DPS against your last run and your best at the same second.

Stop hitting for a few seconds and the run is over; hit again for the next one. Every run is kept with your rotation, combat power, item level, stats, buffs and gear — the last 100 per character. On the browser page, Summary → Analysis ↗ puts two runs side by side and marks what changed, down to the gear slot you swapped. The meter overlay shows the training card too while you train.

While you hit, the card tells you when you stood still without a skill or left a skill ready unused, and draws your run against your best one; the voice counts you in and says how the run went (switch it off under Warnings → Training voice). A finished run shows your opener against the rest, crit and back-attack rates, your buffs’ uptime and the average of your last five runs. On the Analysis page you can give a run a label (“new gloves”) and compare the labels.

Stats come from the game when you log in or change zone, not when you swap gear: change zone once after swapping so the comparison has the new numbers. If you started A2Naki while already in the game, the training card still works, and your runs are given to your character at your next zone change or teleport.

I hit the dummy but no training card shows

Check three things:

  • Which screen? The training card sits where the target card is: on the Xeneon Edge, on the browser page and, while you train, at the top of the meter overlay.
  • An old browser tab? A browser page opened before you updated A2Naki keeps the old version. From 0.2.24 the page reloads by itself after an update; before that, press F5 once.
  • Which dummy? Punching Bags and Training Scarecrows count. Another kind of dummy? Send us a bug report with a short recording (see below).
What do the spoken boss warnings say, and can I change them?

Every call per boss is in the dungeon boss guides; how it works is on the boss mod page.

On bosses the app knows, the voice says short phrases at the right moment — “Avoid!”, “Stop DPS, run away from boss!”, “Kael marked, move to Kael!”, “Stagger!” — and the same line shows on the target card. Some come a little early (“Stop DPS soon!”).

Gear button → Warnings: voice on or off, volume, speed, how many seconds ahead, and one switch per kind of warning. Training voice is the countdown and the result on the training dummy.

Count boss casts (off by default) is for learning a new boss: every spell the boss casts gets a number the first time — 1, 2, 3 — and keeps it, and the voice says the number each time it comes. Tell us in the beta channel “3 should say Get out” and the next version says it.

How do I report a bug with a recording?

Tray icon → Start recording, play until the problem happens (a minute is often enough), then tray icon → Create bug report… with Include the recording ticked. Click Send to A2Naki and post the id it shows in the beta channel — or upload the zip from your Desktop with the link pinned there. Recordings are too big for a Discord message.

A recording holds the game traffic around you, including the names of players nearby; it stays on your PC unless you send it, and it is deleted after 7 days.

How do I get the widgets into iCUE?

At the end of setup, click Import all three widgets into iCUE… (or later from the tray menu). iCUE asks once per widget — click Import each time, and the next one opens by itself.

Importing only adds them to iCUE’s widget list (group “A2Naki”). Then create three pages on the Edge, drag one page widget onto each, and set its size to XL under Appearance → Size. You need iCUE 5.47 or newer.

What data is stored, and where?

Everything stays on your PC. The app has no account, no telemetry and sends nothing over the internet.

Settings
%APPDATA%\A2Naki\settings.json
Widget settings
%APPDATA%\A2Naki\widget-prefs.json — theme, language, chosen character
Bridge token
%APPDATA%\A2Naki\bridge-token.txt — readable only by your Windows user
What it learned
Fight history, boss patterns, buffs, crafting levels — in %APPDATA%\A2Naki
Logs
%LOCALAPPDATA%\A2Naki\logs — last 7 days
Recordings
%LOCALAPPDATA%\A2Naki\recordings — only when you switch recording on

A bug report is a zip the app writes to your Desktop (logs, version, settings — never the token). It uploads nothing; you decide whether to send it.

Which languages?

The dashboard speaks English, Greek, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese (BR), Russian, Japanese, Korean and Chinese (Simplified) — gear button → Settings → Language.

Part names stay English, as in the game, and the spoken boss warnings are English only. The beta itself runs in English.
